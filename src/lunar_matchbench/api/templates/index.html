How to read this

What the console shows you

1 · Find the same ground twice

Chandrayaan-2 and NASA's LRO both photographed the whole Moon, years apart, at different resolutions and sun angles. The engine locates your coordinate in each mission's imagery independently.

2 · Lock onto the right scan line

Both cameras are pushbroom scanners: an LROC image is tens of thousands of single lines. The rail on the left shows which line was chosen, how far it drifted from the orbital-geometry estimate, and how much of the strip was searched. Drag it to look anywhere in the product.

3 · Match, then throw out the bad matches

Thousands of candidate correspondences are proposed, then MAGSAC++ keeps only those that agree on one consistent transform. Kept points are solid discs, rejected ones hollow rings.

4 · Judge the result honestly

Every figure comes from the run itself. A failure says whether the whole strip was searched — a real mismatch — or only part of it, which proves nothing either way.

Inlier tie-points
Correspondences that survived geometric verification. More is better; these are what the alignment rests on.
Reprojection RMSE
Average pixel error when a kept point is pushed through the fitted transform. Below about 2 px is a good cross-mission result.
Spatial coverage
How much of the patch has tie-points in it. High coverage means the alignment holds across the whole frame, not just one crater.
Scan-line lock
Whether the reference position was confirmed visually or is only the orbital-geometry estimate. An unverified lock makes any failure inconclusive.
